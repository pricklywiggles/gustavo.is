# The palette

One warm ramp, one cool branch reserved for a single section, and a semantic layer mapped
onto the ramp. Every value on this page is read from `src/lib/ramp.ts` at render time, and
`src/app/design-tokens.test.ts` fails the suite when that file, `src/app/globals.css`, and
`DESIGN.md`'s front matter disagree. So the swatches cannot go stale; only the prose can.

Source of truth: the `@theme` block in `src/app/globals.css`. Narrative: `DESIGN.md`.

## The one ramp

Every warm token sorted by OKLCH lightness, palest first. Hover a step to read it, click to
copy the hex. Warning Ember sits in the strip by lightness but belongs to none of it: at
C 0.245 it is the one colour that leaves the warm family on purpose.

<RampStrip />

<Rule title="The One Ramp Rule">
	Every surface colour, from the palest background to the darkest text, comes from this
	single warm ramp. The cool branch is the one scoped exception and may not grow. If a new
	colour does not sit on the ramp it does not belong on the site, including any future dark
	mode, which this system does not have.
</Rule>

<Rule title="The Rare Blaze Rule">
	Horizon Blaze is a focus and data colour, not a decoration. It shows up on a ring, a
	chart line, a rare accent, never as a background flood.
</Rule>

<Rule title="The Unbroken Ground Rule">
	Every page closes on Dusk Earth and hands off to the Dusk Earth footer with no visible
	seam. The landing page's landfall vista, the blog's `main`, and `SiteFooter` are all the
	same ground.
</Rule>

## Type roles

Three role variables in `@theme static`, resolving to two faces. Roles are named by usage,
not by face, so the dev-only font lab can repoint them inline on `html` without touching a
component. `static` is what forces all three into `:root` even when a role has no utility in
the source yet.

| Role | Face | Weights | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--font-sans` | Wotfard | 100 to 700 loaded, 400 to 700 in practice | Body copy, labels, form text, UI copy. Applied to `html` in the base layer. |
| `--font-display` | Kitora Bold | 700 | Every title-like and display role: the landing equation, section titles, page and reading-page headings. |
| `--font-legend` | Kitora Bold | 700 | Instrument text: panorama roles, the year, the counter and its label. |

Kitora ships one bold cut, so hierarchy inside the title voice comes from size alone.

| Step | Face and weight | Size | Line height | Tracking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | Kitora 700 | clamp(2.25rem, 4.5vw, 3.5rem) | 1.05 to 1.1 | -0.01em |
| Headline | Kitora 700 | clamp(1.875rem, 3vw, 2.25rem) | 1.15 | normal |
| Title | Kitora 700 | 1.25rem | 1.3 | normal |
| Legend | Kitora 700 | 0.8125rem | 1.4 | 0.01em |
| Body | Wotfard 400 | 1rem | 1.6 | normal |
| Label | Wotfard 500 | 0.8125rem | 1.4 | 0.01em |

<Note>
	<p>
		<strong>Kitora carries two roles, not one.</strong> Display and legend both resolve to
		the same face today. They are separate variables so the instrument text can diverge
		later without dragging headings with it.
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>Kitora needs two hand-tuned corrections</strong>, both in the base layer of
		`globals.css`. Its word space is 0.366em against Wotfard's 0.233em, so `.font-display`
		pulls word-spacing back to about 0.27em. And its equals sign draws as a single bar that
		reads as a minus, so `.kitora-equals` stacks a second hyphen with hand-tuned offsets.
		Both are calibrated to this face. Swapping the display face means re-measuring both in
		the font lab.
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>The blog holdout is settled.</strong> The reading page's headings take Kitora;
		the blog index's entry titles stay Wotfard medium on purpose, because the entry list
		reads as UI rather than as titles.
	</p>
</Note>

## Focus rings

The ring is picked by the ground it lands on, never by habit. Both live in
`src/lib/focus-ring.ts`, keyed by `Tone`, and every recipe that renders a control requires
one: `cta`, `navPill`, `textLink`, and the bar themes, whose `text` is narrowed per ground
so a theme cannot pair light labels with a light ring.

| Tone | Ring | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dark | <Dot token="horizon-blaze" />opaque Horizon Blaze | 6.41:1 on Dusk Ink, 3.20:1 on Dusk Earth |
| light | <Dot token="canyon-brown" />opaque Canyon Brown | 3.77:1 on Pale Dune, 4.04:1 on Sand Haze, 4.47:1 on First Light |

`FOCUS_RING` hugs controls that carry their own padding (buttons, pills, inputs, media
boxes). `FOCUS_OUTLINE` is the same colour stepped 3px out, for bare text and glyphs
(footer links, menu links, tabs, the logo) where a hugging ring crowds the letters.

<Rule title="The Ground Picks the Ring Rule">
	A hand-written ring class is a bug, not a variant. Blaze on Pale Dune measures 1.89:1 and
	is not an indicator; the `/50` blaze tint the dark ring once wore fell to 2.63:1 on Dusk
	Ink and was retired for the same reason.
</Rule>

<Note>
	<p>
		<strong>This layer renders nowhere but here.</strong> `src/components/ui/button.tsx` is
		its only reader, and nothing imports that button, so the semantic tokens are the base
		for future UI rather than live chrome. The base-layer rules are the exception: they
		make `--background`, `--foreground`, and `--border` real on every page.
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>The base layer's default outline is off-system.</strong> The shadcn preset's
		`* &#123; @apply outline-ring/50 &#125;` is still there, and blaze at `/50` measures
		2.63:1 on Dusk Ink, under the 3:1 indicator floor. Every real control supersedes it
		with `FOCUS_RING` or `FOCUS_OUTLINE`, so anything actually falling back to it is a bug.
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>The gray layer is gone.</strong> Twenty tokens from the shadcn preset (card,
		popover, accent, muted-foreground, five charts, eight sidebar tokens) were chroma-0
		grays with no consumers. Deleted. When a future copy-in needs one, re-add it mapped to
		a ramp colour, never a gray.
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>Dark mode is neutered by construction.</strong> The `@custom-variant dark` line
		scopes `dark:` to a `.dark` class nothing ever applies, so dark-mode classes arriving
		with future shadcn copy-ins are inert instead of activating on OS preference.
	</p>
</Note>

## The overlay ladder

Four named steps, tokens in `globals.css` and utilities on top of them. Never hand-write a
z-index on fixed or sticky chrome.

| Token | Utility | Value | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--z-index-bar` | `z-bar` | 50 | Every site bar: the riding header, the return header, the blog's sticky sky bar |
| `--z-index-overlay` | `z-overlay` | 60 | Full-viewport sheets and scrims that cover the bar: the mobile menu, the contact dialog's backdrop |
| `--z-index-dialog` | `z-dialog` | 70 | The modal panel above its scrim. The step names the panel, not the ARIA role: the mobile menu is `role="dialog"` and sits at `overlay` |
| `--z-index-dev` | `z-dev` | 100 | Development-only chrome (the font lab). Never reaches a visitor |

Page-internal layering (`z-0` to `z-30`, `-z-1`) is local to a section and must stay under
`bar`. The Motion+ blinds curtain paints at its own library-owned 999999, above the ladder.

## Tints

The palette's second dimension: translucent steps of ramp colours doing the work of muted
text, hairlines, and scrims. 83 utilities in `src/` carry an opacity modifier across 18
distinct steps, 41 of them on the ladder below. The shipped values were vetted visually and
stay as they are; the ladder is the guidance for new code.

| Step | Role | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/10` | Scrim, faint surface lift | `bg-pale-dune/10` |
| `/30` | Hairline, quiet border | `border-dusk-earth/30` |
| `/50` | Mid tint | `bg-dusk-ink/50` |
| `/75` | Secondary text | `text-first-light/75` |
| `/85` | Primary text as a tint | `text-first-light/85` |

<Note>
	<p>
		<strong>Contrast-check every text tint against its real background.</strong> The steps
		are not interchangeable: `text-pale-dune/85` on Dusk Earth holds AA at 4.88:1, but
		`/75` drops to 4.21:1 and fails. When rounding toward the ladder would cross 4.5:1,
		take the step up, not down.
	</p>
	<p>
		<strong>No wholesale renormalization.</strong> A sweep moving the 42 off-ladder call
		sites onto the ladder would change vetted pixels across the site for no visible
		benefit. The ladder constrains new code; the existing values are the record of what was
		tuned by eye.
	</p>
</Note>

## Surface themes

A section declares its background with `data-surface`. The return header probes the visual
stack, finds the declaring element, and renders one ramp step off that surface. The table
lives in `src/components/bar-themes.ts`. Every new full-bleed section has to declare one,
and an unknown value falls back to first-light.

| data-surface | Bar | Label text | Hover pill | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Dot token="first-light" />`first-light` | sand-haze/90 + blur | dusk-earth, 6.4:1 | pale-dune | light |
| <Dot token="pale-dune" />`pale-dune` | dusk-earth/90 + blur | pale-dune | canyon-brown | dark |
| <Dot token="dusk-ink" />`dusk-ink` | dusk-earth/90 + blur | pale-dune | canyon-brown | dark |
| <Dot token="dusk-earth" />`dusk-earth` | dusk-earth/90 + blur | pale-dune | canyon-brown | dark |
| <Dot token="day-sky" />`day-sky` | dusk-ink/80 + blur | pale-dune | dusk-earth | dark |
| <Dot token="pale-dune" />`SKY_BAR` | pale-dune, opaque | dusk-ink | amber-mirage | light |

The three middle rows share one `DARK_BAR` object. It is dark on purpose: over bright
surfaces a light bar measured 1.06:1 against the section and the backdrop blur smeared the
headline through it. Dusk Earth gives 4.83:1 for the bar and 5.72:1 for its labels. Day Sky
gets its own dark theme because a warm light bar clashes with the cool surface. SKY_BAR is
opaque and carries no blur, so the blog's sticky bar does not pay for continuous backdrop
compositing.

`colorReplacements` swaps the hexes back out for `var(--color-*)` after highlighting.

## The reading page's prose ladder

`.blog-prose` in `globals.css` remaps the typography plugin onto the dark-earth ground. It
is left unlayered so it beats the plugin's layered utilities without `!important`.

| Prose role | Token | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body, headings, bold, quotes, code | <Dot token="pale-dune" />Pale Dune | 6.03:1 on Dusk Earth |
| Links, ordered-list counters, the h2 period, the rule's dot | <Dot token="noon-sun" />Noon Sun | Counters read as text, so they take the 4.5:1 floor and hold 5.08:1 |
| Bullets, quote borders, the code header strip | <Dot token="dune-tan" />Dune Tan | Glyphs take the 3:1 floor and hold 3.77:1 |
| Code block background, inline code chips | <Dot token="dusk-ink" />Dusk Ink | The site's darkest ground, one step below the page |

Hairlines and scrims here (Pale Dune at 15, 25, and 30 percent) sit off the tint ladder on
purpose, reviewed and kept as non-text decoration.

## Keeping this page honest

Swatch values, hexes, and every contrast number are computed from `src/lib/ramp.ts` at
render, and `design-tokens.test.ts` holds that file, the `@theme` block, and DESIGN.md's
front matter in verbatim sync, both directions. Adding a token to `globals.css` without
describing it here fails the suite; describing one that no longer exists fails it too.

The prose is hand-maintained and can still drift. Reference counts used to live on the
swatches and were the first thing to go stale, so each token now names where it is used
instead.

sRGB hex is projected from OKLCH through Oklab and gamma-encoded, then clamped to gamut.
Warning Ember and Deep Sea fall outside sRGB, so their hex is a clipped approximation and a
P3 display renders them more saturated than the swatch here.
